Historical timeline

Selected milestones in semiconductor development. History involves many laboratories and contributors; this timeline is an overview only. For a fuller published survey, see Łukasiak & Jakubowski (2010) on the sources page.

  1. 1833

    Early observation

    Michael Faraday noted that silver sulfide behaved differently from metals when heated. A complete theory of semiconductors developed much later.

  2. 1947

    Transistor at Bell Labs

    John Bardeen and Walter Brattain demonstrated a point-contact transistor in germanium. William Shockley advanced junction transistors. Solid-state devices began replacing vacuum tubes in many applications.

  3. 1954

    Silicon transistors

    Texas Instruments commercialized silicon transistors. Silicon’s oxide layer later supported reliable surfaces and metal-oxide-semiconductor (MOS) devices.

  4. 1958–1959

    Integrated circuit

    Jack Kilby (Texas Instruments) and Robert Noyce (Fairchild) demonstrated multiple devices and interconnects on a single substrate—early integrated circuits (ICs).

  5. 1965

    Moore’s article

    Gordon Moore published on how many components might fit on one chip. The industry later treated that trend as an informal roadmap (Moore’s law).

  6. 1971

    Microprocessor

    Intel 4004: central processing unit (CPU) logic integrated on one MOS chip.

  7. 1970s–1980s

    CMOS dominance

    Complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) design pairs n-type and p-type transistors to reduce static power when idle; it became the standard for digital logic.

  8. 1990s–2000s

    Copper interconnects

    Copper wiring and low-k dielectric insulators reduced signal delay in on-chip connections, not only in the transistor itself.

  9. 2010s–present

    FinFET, EUV, and chiplets

    Three-dimensional transistor shapes (fin field-effect transistors, or FinFETs), extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography for the smallest features, and advanced packaging (including chiplets—smaller dies combined in one package) when monolithic scaling becomes harder.
